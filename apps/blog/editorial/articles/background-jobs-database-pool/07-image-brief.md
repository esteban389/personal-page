## Selected trial prompt

Create a restrained photographic editorial still life for a Java architectural incident article. Landscape 16:9, preferably 2048×1152 or larger. A small brass key rack with visibly empty hooks on a warm gray wall above a dark wooden school-office desk. Directly beneath the rack, borrowed brass keys lie clearly visible on a thick stack of plain muted-blue school folders. Beside that stack is one thin cream folder waiting, without a key. Keep the rack, borrowed keys and waiting folder together in the central half of the frame. Soft natural side light, realistic tactile paper and brass, quiet understated composition. Leave the lower third as uncluttered dark desk for a white title overlay added by the website. No typography, labels, logos, people, student information, glowing technology, locks, broken objects or missing/lost keys. Communicate that all available keys have been borrowed by one workload while another waits; metaphor only, no literal architecture diagram or numerical count.

Crop-safe focal region: central half above quiet lower third. Provisional alt intent: empty key rack above borrowed keys on stacked folders, beside a separate waiting folder. Final alt and crop approval await generated asset inspection.

## 1. All the keys are checked out — recommended

- Core idea: an empty shared key rack makes exhausted borrowable capacity visible.
- Claim: background and HTTP work need the same finite connections.
- Subject/focal point: small brass key rack with all hooks empty; a cluster of keys beside a thick pile of plain school folders; one thin folder waiting nearby.
- Composition: restrained editorial still life, rack and keys in central half, warm gray wall, muted blue folders, soft side light, tactile brass and paper. Calm dark desk across lower third.
- Crop safety: rack, keys and waiting folder kept close together; no essential content at edges. Wide landscape minimum1600×900; portrait-like crops still show rack.
- Avoid implying: actual keys, a connection leak, lost grades, ownership forever, or a specific connection count. Borrowed keys are visible rather than lost.
- Failure risks: could read as lost-key story; folder/borrowed-key relationship must remain legible. No numbers, labels, logos or text.
- Provisional alt intent: empty key rack, borrowed keys beside a stack of folders, and a separate folder waiting.
- Why choose: communicates scarce shared capacity with few objects.

## 2. The report pile has taken the whole desk

- Core idea: background work occupies a shared workspace until everyday work has nowhere to happen.
- Claim: work performed for reports competes with ordinary use of the app.
- Subject/focal point: one small school-office desk covered by a dense stack of plain report folders, with a single open grade sheet crowded at the center edge.
- Composition: overhead editorial photograph or tactile illustration, warm wood, cream paper, restrained blue accents, diffuse daylight; lower third uncluttered desk surface.
- Crop safety: keep collision between stack and single sheet at center, silhouettes readable without page details.
- Avoid implying: lost student data, actual leaked documents, teachers at fault, or paper storage/memory as diagnosed bottleneck.
- Failure risks: generic paperwork/bureaucracy image; less exact about database capacity than option1. Use blank abstract ruling only, no student names.
- Provisional alt intent: report folders occupy a small desk beside a single grade sheet.
- Why choose: closest to the school setting and friendlier in tone.

## 3. A feeder releases a few cards at a time

- Core idea: controlled admission makes a large backlog manageable.
- Claim: batches and shared limits control work entering constrained execution.
- Subject/focal point: compact tabletop card-feeding mechanism; stacked blue cards upstream, a visible stop allowing a small group through to a narrow working area.
- Composition: restrained physical-model illustration, matte off-white housing, blue paper, soft directional light; single central gate; dark quiet lower third.
- Crop safety: gate and both sides of its immediate path fit central half; upstream stack stays small enough to survive narrow crop.
- Avoid implying: all paths use one scheduler, zero queuing, exact batch count, or physical mechanical components in production.
- Failure risks: generic factory metaphor or complicated meaningless machine; keep mechanism simple, no gears, glowing parts or cables.
- Provisional alt intent: a small feeder holds a stack of cards while releasing a few into a work area.
- Why choose: illustrates the mitigation rather than the outage.
